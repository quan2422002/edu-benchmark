F01 rapid evidence review protocol
Status
LOCKED_AT_IMPLEMENTATION_START


Search cut-off: 2026-06-21.


This is an expanded rapid evidence review, not a full systematic review.
Review questions
1. Which tutoring capabilities are evaluated in LLM/AI tutor research?
2. What task inputs, outputs, interaction modes and rubrics are used?
3. How are explanation, diagnosis, feedback, hinting, scaffolding and multi-turn behavior assessed?
4. How does Computer Science education represent misconceptions, algorithms, debugging and code feedback?
5. What roles do teachers and other human experts play in authoring, annotation, calibration, review and adjudication?
6. What reliability or validity evidence is reported for human and automatic evaluation?
7. What failure modes affect automatic metrics and LLM judges?
8. What evidence exists for middle-school, multilingual, low-resource or Vietnamese education?
Population, domain and outcomes
* Population: learners and teachers in school or higher-education settings, with priority for secondary learners.
* Domain: tutoring generally plus Computer Science/programming education.
* Intervention/system: LLM tutor, AI tutor, intelligent tutoring system, automated feedback system or evaluator relevant to open-ended tutoring.
* Outcomes: observable tutoring quality, feedback quality, diagnostic accuracy, learner agency, task/rubric validity, human agreement and evaluation reliability. Learning outcomes are recorded separately and are not inferred from response-quality results.
Evidence clusters
1. LLM/AI tutoring benchmarks.
2. Feedback, scaffolding, hinting, student-state diagnosis and multi-turn tutoring.
3. Computer Science/programming misconceptions, debugging and feedback.
4. Human rubric design, teacher annotation and inter-rater reliability.
5. Automatic evaluation and LLM-as-a-judge validity, bias and failure modes.
6. Multilingual, low-resource and middle-school evidence.
Sources
* ACL Anthology;
* ACM Digital Library;
* IEEE Xplore;
* ERIC;

* Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex only for discovery and citation chaining.


Google Scholar may support discovery, but final claims must point to an original paper, proceedings record or publisher page.

Inclusion criteria
* Original study, benchmark, dataset, review or evaluation method with enough methodological detail to extract task, rubric, metric or human role.
* Direct relevance to tutoring behavior, educational feedback, learner-state diagnosis, CS/programming support, rubric/reliability or educational judge validity.
* Stable URL or DOI.
* English or Vietnamese full text/extended abstract available for core extraction.
* Foundational pre-2018 studies are allowed only when directly relevant to tutoring feedback, rubric or reliability.
Exclusion criteria
* Opinion/blog without research method.
* Pure factual QA with no tutoring behavior.
* Tutor system paper without relevant evaluation detail.
* Duplicate/preprint superseded by a suitable published version.
* Claim not traceable to the original source.
* Generic LLM judge research without a defensible educational-evaluation implication is background only.
Screening and extraction
* Screen at least 50 unique candidates by title/abstract.
* Target 20–30 full-text core records.
* Record one explicit full-text exclusion reason.
* Resolve duplicates by DOI, normalized title and known paper versions.
* Extract using the canonical research-methodologist evidence schema.
* Record section, page, table or figure for important evidence.
* Use short paraphrases rather than long quotation.
Synthesis labels
* evidence: directly supported by an extracted source.
* inference: reasoned implication that extends beyond the source.
* open_question: requires additional research or human judgment.
Stopping rule
Stop when all six clusters have been searched systematically and two successive search/citation-chaining rounds add no important task family or rubric dimension. If the deadline is reached first, document unsaturated clusters and do not claim comprehensive coverage.
Quality controls
* Audit at least 20% of included extractions.
* Trace at least five synthesis claims back to matrix and source location.
* Label publication status.
* Keep learning outcomes separate from response-quality evidence.
* Report cross-domain, learner-level and language limitations.
* Route pedagogical and curriculum implications to expert teachers.
